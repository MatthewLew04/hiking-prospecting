## What is happening

The Northwest is in its biggest mining upswing since the early 1990s, and it is running on two engines at once. The first is gold at record prices — Paramount's Grassy Mountain feasibility study now uses a $3,600/oz price deck — which is reviving the classic districts: Stibnite is under construction, the Silver Valley is producing at records, Black Pine is in EIS, and money is re-staking historic gold ground from Boise Basin to South Pass. The second engine is the critical-minerals scramble set off by China's late-2024 antimony export ban and the Russian uranium ban: antimony smelting at Thompson Falls now carries a $245M defense stockpile contract, Wyoming's in-situ uranium plants are restarting one by one, and rare earths are being pulled out of a brand-new Wyoming coal mine. Washington sits out the boom entirely — it has not had an operating metal mine since Buckhorn closed in 2017.

The claims data captures the moment better than any press release. Across the five states there are 113,330 active claims, and 33,689 of them — 30% — are in FILED status, meaning they were staked so recently that BLM has not finished processing them. That number is the region's pulse, and it is not evenly distributed: Idaho is at 46% filed (18,856 pending claims — a genuine staking rush), Wyoming 30%, Oregon 20%, and Montana just 1% (18,335 of its 18,520 claims are settled holdings; Montana's ground is held, not contested). Behind it all sit 819,158 closed claims — every piece of ground someone once believed in — which is the historical memory the map mines for opportunity.

## What is interesting (the cross-reference findings)

Cross-referencing the six layers produces signals no single source shows. First, **convergence validates the headlines**: Stibnite–Yellow Pine has 3,070 active claims within 25 km — the largest cluster in the entire five-state region — exactly where a $2.9B financed mine is being built, and the Coeur d'Alene district pairs 2,208 active claims with 1,772 mapped historic workings, the densest workings-plus-claims combination anywhere. When the past (USMIN workings), the present (active claims), and the news all point at the same hillside, the story is real. Second, **the data finds stories with no news flow**: South Pass, Wyoming shows 960 freshly filed claims — the largest new-staking signal of any district, ahead of Stibnite — with essentially zero press coverage. Third, **the inverse signal — orphan ground**: districts thick with historic workings but nearly bare of active claims. Canyon Creek, Oregon (221 workings, 1,564 closed claims, only 40 active) and the Stokes district in Cassia County (40 workings, 44 active) are proven-dug, largely open ground — the purest prospecting leads this project has produced. Fourth, **the Cassia spillover**: the four researched Cassia districts each show 410–460 pending filings within 25 km, meaning the staking wave around Liberty Gold's Black Pine is spreading across Cody's home county right now. And a data-hygiene note that doubles as a finding: Oregon reports 3,163 MRDS "producers," but the bulk are sand-and-gravel operations — commodity filters matter before drawing conclusions.

## The Top 10

1. **Stibnite is actually being built (ID).** Ground broken Oct 2025, $2.9B EXIM loan closed May 21, 2026, injunction denied May 29, Burntlog road underway, operations targeted 2029 — the first US mined antimony since 1997. Data: 3,070 active / 709 filed claims within 25 km, the region's #1 cluster. ([Perpetua](https://perpetuaresources.com/))
2. **Silver Valley's full-blown revival (ID).** Bunker Hill poured its first concentrate in 45 years on June 29, 2026; Lucky Friday set an all-time record (5.3 Moz Ag, 2025); Galena is expanding to 1,200 tpd. Data: 1,772 historic workings + 2,208 active + 736 filed claims — every layer lit at once.
3. **The Idaho staking rush (statewide).** 18,856 of 40,624 active claims (46%) are pending adjudication — no other state is above 30%. Visible only in the claims data.
4. **Black Pine (Cassia County, ID).** EIS scoping opened Apr 3, 2026 (FAST-41); feasibility study Q4 2026; first gold targeted 2028; the staking spillover is measurably spreading through the county's other districts. ([Liberty Gold](https://libertygold.ca/projects/black-pine/))
5. **Wyoming uranium is back on.** Shirley Basin started up Apr 2026, Lost Creek captured 110,314 lb in Q1, and US Q1-2026 output was the best since 2015. WY's 40,873 active claims essentially tie Idaho for the regional lead.
6. **Rare earths arrive via a coal mine (WY/MT).** Ramaco's Brook Mine (opened July 2025) feeds a pilot REE-oxide plant coming online in 2026, with a $200M expansion announced July 25, 2026; Montana's Sheep Creek carries FAST-41 status but still hasn't drilled — enthusiasm ahead of evidence.
7. **The antimony scramble (MT).** Post-ban prices tripled; US Antimony's Thompson Falls smelter holds a $245M DLA stockpile contract, commissioned a new smelter Q2 2026, and is reopening Montana antimony mining.
8. **South Pass: the quiet gold rush (WY).** 960 freshly filed claims — the region's single biggest new-staking signal — at the 1867 district, with no matching news coverage. The dataset's best "somebody knows something" flag.
9. **Oregon wakes up.** Grassy Mountain received the first federal ROD for a modern Oregon gold mine (Jan 29, 2026; state permits pending), and McDermitt's 21.5 Mt LCE stands as the largest US lithium resource, heading for a late-2026 listing.
10. **Orphan ground.** Canyon Creek OR and Stokes ID: dense historic workings, almost no live claims. For a prospector, the most actionable item on this list — after verifying land status and withdrawals on the ground.

**Watchlist:** Stillwater PGMs stabilizing behind a preliminary 132% Russian-palladium duty; Hecla's Libby Exploration Project cleared federal permitting July 9, 2026 (litigation pending); Washington's continuing shutout — a 24:1 closed-to-active claim ratio, the most extinguished ground in the region.
