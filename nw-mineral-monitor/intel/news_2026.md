## 1. Perpetua Resources — Stibnite Gold Project, Valley County ID (gold + antimony)
**Confidence: verified**

Early-works construction began in October 2025 (following the January 2025 ROD), and the 2026 field season is underway: Burntlog Route access-road construction commenced May 30, 2026, alongside worker housing, powerline upgrades and geotechnical drilling, with ~$45 million of direct 2026 work for Idaho contractors; a U.S. District Court denied a preliminary injunction against the project on May 29, 2026. On May 21, 2026 the Export-Import Bank of the United States approved a $2.9 billion, 13-year senior secured loan (upfront facility of $2.4 billion, priced at long-dated Treasuries +100 bp, repayments from 2030), with definitive documentation/financial close expected in H2 2026. Perpetua is targeting operations in 2029, including delivery of antimony to the U.S. military by 2029; Stibnite holds the only identified domestic antimony reserve (company says it could meet ~35% of U.S. antimony demand in early production years). Federal defense support to date totals ~$59.4M under DPA Title III ($24.8M Dec 2022 + $34.6M Feb 2024) plus up to $6.9M awarded in May 2025 for a domestic antimony-trisulfide supply chain.

## 3. Bunker Hill Mine restart, Kellogg ID (Silver Valley) — zinc-lead-silver
**Confidence: verified**

Yes — the restart hit its H1 2026 target: on June 29, 2026 Bunker Hill announced production of first concentrate, the first product shipped from the mine in 45 years, with concentrate going to Teck's Trail smelter in B.C. Commercial production (defined as 90 days at >65% of 1,800 tpd with operating stability) is expected by end of 2026, at which point the company will issue 2027-28 operating guidance. ## 5. Sibanye-Stillwater — Stillwater & East Boulder PGM mines, MT
**Confidence: verified**

Both Montana operations continue mining, but the Stillwater West section remains on care and maintenance following the late-2024 restructuring that cut ~700 jobs (~40% of the workforce); the US PGM business returned to profitability in H2 2025, helped by Section 45X production credits (~$285M recognized through H1 2025) and recovering palladium prices, and Sibanye is targeting AISC of ~$1,000/2E oz within 2-3 years (2E reserves were 19.4 Moz at end-2025). In the trade case Sibanye brought, the Commerce Department's International Trade Administration issued a preliminary antidumping determination (reported Feb 20, 2026) imposing a preliminary 132% duty on Russian palladium, with the countervailing/subsidy portion still pending; palladium traded around $1,600-1,795/oz in February 2026 versus $800-1,000 during the dumping period. Q1 2026 production was 5% lower YoY with AISC up 14%, and no restart date for Stillwater West has been announced; the Stillwater mine expansion was added to the FAST-41 transparency list in May 2025.

## 6. United States Antimony Corp (UAMY) — Thompson Falls MT smelter
**Confidence: verified**

USAC won a $245 million, five-year sole-source Defense Logistics Agency contract (announced early October 2025) to supply antimony ingots for the National Defense Stockpile, and in Q2 2026 delivered its first two shipments (~82,000 lb, ~$2.6M invoiced in June 2026) while commissioning expanded smelting furnaces at a newly constructed Thompson Falls facility; a ~$20M expansion targets ~400 tons/month of capacity (~5x prior levels) and ~25 new jobs (MTPR, Apr 6, 2026). On sourcing, USAC is reactivating mining at Stibnite Hill adjacent to the smelter (Small Miner Exclusion Statement filed July 2025; surface mining reported underway by April 2026) and has built a large Alaska claims position (144 claims/~8,998 acres in the Fairbanks area as of Apr 7, 2025, and 35,000+ total Alaska acres per its July 2025 release, plus Stibnite Creek and Maclaren River claims). Antimony prices have cooled from post-China-export-ban records: ~$51.8/kg in late July 2026, down ~45% from the ~$94.85/kg peak roughly a year earlier.

## 7. US Critical Materials — Sheep Creek REE-gallium project, Ravalli County MT
**Confidence: partial**

Sheep Creek was added to the FAST-41 transparency list on Nov 19, 2025 (with USFS as lead agency; the listing describes gallium plus neodymium/praseodymium/niobium targets spanning southwest Montana and east-central Idaho), and on April 9, 2026 the company announced receipt of a USGS technical report from a 45,506 line-km airborne magnetic/radiometric survey flown Oct 2023-Sep 2024. However, as of late November 2025 local reporting found no exploration drilling had ever occurred (surface sampling only) and no Plan of Operations had been submitted to the Bitterroot National Forest; the company's own 2025-26 strategy emphasizes pilot beneficiation and an INL Phase II CRADA on REE/gallium separation, with production still years away. ## 8. Ramaco Resources — Brook Mine, Sheridan County WY (coal + rare earths)
**Confidence: verified**

The Brook Mine opened with a ribbon-cutting on July 11, 2025 — billed as the first new U.S. rare earth mine in ~70 years and Wyoming's first new coal mine in ~50 years — and Ramaco broke ground on its pilot processing plant and lab adjacent to the mine on Oct 23, 2025, supported by a $6.1M Wyoming Energy Authority matching grant. On July 25, 2026 Ramaco announced an accelerated expansion: target oxide output raised to ~3,400 tons/year of REE/critical-mineral oxides (from 1,240), coal production rising from 2 to 5 Mtpa (permit area expanding from 4,500 to ~16,000 acres), the pilot plant to begin operating later in 2026, and construction of a full-scale commercial oxide plant targeted to start in 2026 (18-month build), backed by a $200M equity raise and a credit facility upsized to $400M. Note that Ramaco's rare-earth grade and economics claims drew public scrutiny from some geologists and short-sellers in late 2025 (Cowboy State Daily, Nov 16, 2025).

## 12. Jindalee Lithium — McDermitt project, Malheur County OR (McDermitt caldera)
**Confidence: verified**

Jindalee's McDermitt project at the northern (Oregon) end of the McDermitt caldera hosts a 2025-updated mineral resource of 21.5 Mt of lithium carbonate equivalent — the largest lithium deposit in the U.S. by contained LCE — and a late-2024 pre-feasibility study outlined a mine life exceeding 60 years using only part of the resource. The McDermitt Exploration Project was among the first ten FAST-41 transparency projects announced April 18, 2025. In 2026 Jindalee is spinning its U.S. assets into "US Elemental" via a SPAC merger targeting a Nasdaq listing in Q3-Q4 2026 (Jindalee retaining ~80%), with H2 2026 infill drilling and the start of a feasibility study targeted for completion by end-2027 and federal permits by end-2028.

## 13. Hecla — Montanore (Libby Exploration Project) / Rock Creek, Libby MT area
**Confidence: verified**

The U.S. Forest Service approved Hecla's Libby Exploration Project — the reconfigured evaluation phase of the Montanore copper-silver project — in October 2025, and on July 9, 2026 the Federal Permitting Council announced the project had completed federal permitting (the 19th mining project to do so under the current administration; it was a FAST-41 transparency project). The plan is to dewater the existing adit and extend it 4,200 ft plus ~6,300 ft of new tunnels for a multi-year underground evaluation (about 16 years including reclamation, employing 30-35). Six conservation groups represented by Earthjustice sued over the approval in federal court in Missoula on about April 1, 2026, citing grizzly bear, bull trout and Cabinet Mountains Wilderness water impacts. Rock Creek (Sanders County) remains on hold — Hecla has said it will not pursue exploration permits there until Libby/Montanore advances.

## 14. Kinross Buckhorn/Kettle River WA legacy; WA metal mining in 2026
**Confidence: partial**

The Okanogan Highlands Alliance's Clean Water Act citizen suit settled on November 1, 2024: Crown Resources/Kinross will fund $3 million in upper-Columbia conservation projects and undertake pollutant-source investigations and a corrective action plan, after a 2022 federal ruling found 3,000+ permit violations (Crown acknowledged 3,500+ monthly violations, over 100,000 violation-days, Feb 2015-Jul 2022); Washington State's parallel case was not part of that settlement and was still ongoing at the time — no 2025-26 resolution of the state case could be verified. Reverse-osmosis water treatment at the closed mine has run since 2014 and long-term treatment/monitoring obligations continue. Buckhorn operated 2008-2017 and was Washington's last producing gold mine; no operating metal mine in Washington in 2026 could be identified (WA DNR's mining page was inaccessible for direct confirmation, and no restart announcements were found).
