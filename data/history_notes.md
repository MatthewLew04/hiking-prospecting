# Mining History of Cassia County, Idaho — Research Notes

Compiled 2026-07-29. County seat Burley; towns Oakley, Albion, Elba, Almo, Malta, Declo.
County FIPS 16031; bbox −114.2867/41.9882 to −112.9999/42.6878; borders Box Elder Co. UT and Elko Co. NV on the south.

---

## 1. County mining overview

Cassia County has never been a famous metal-mining county. The Idaho Geological Survey's statewide
Mines and Prospects database (DD-1, v.1.2023) contains **125 records for Cassia County** out of ~9,400
statewide, and Anderson (1931) summed up the metal story in one line: *"Ore was discovered in the
district about 1880, but the production has been small."* The county's mineral economy has instead
been defined by four quite different stories:

1. **Small silver–lead–zinc lode districts** discovered ca. 1880 in two separated ranges — the
   **Stokes district** (Albion Range) and the **Black Pine district** (Black Pine Mountains) — plus a
   1920s borderland silver play (the **Dolomite district**) tied to Utah's famous Vipont mine.
2. **Fine "flour gold" placers** on the Snake River bars (1880s–1905) and lower Raft River.
3. **Industrial and energy minerals**: Goose Creek lignite (briefly mined, later a 1950s AEC uranium
   study area), marble/lime, brick clay near Burley, pumicite, and — by far the most commercially
   important mining ever done in the county — the **Oakley stone** micaceous-quartzite dimension-stone
   quarries on Middle Mountain, continuously active since 1948.
4. **The modern Black Pine revival**: Pegasus Gold's open-pit heap-leach mine (1991–1997, 435,000 oz
   Au) and Liberty Gold's multi-million-ounce **Black Pine Oxide Gold Project**, in federal/state
   permitting as of mid-2026 with first gold targeted for 2028 — potentially the largest mine in
   county history.

Districts named in the Idaho Mine Inspector's annual reports for Cassia County (1921–1955):
**Stokes, Black Pine, Dolomite, Grape Creek, Snake River (placer), Unorganized.**
IGS DD-1 today tags Cassia records with districts **Black Pine (16), Goose Creek (16), Stokes (2)**;
most records carry no district tag.

> **Do-not-conflate warnings:**
> - The **Black Pine mine, Lemhi County** (gold, reopened 1952; near Gilmore) is a *different* property
>   from Cassia County's Black Pine district. 1950s Inspector reports mention both.
> - The **Vipont mine is in Box Elder County, Utah** (Ashbrook district, Grouse Creek Mtns), not Idaho —
>   verified via Western Mining History/MRDS and by its absence from the IGS Idaho database. Its supply
>   town was Oakley, Idaho, and its belt crosses into Cassia County as the "Dolomite district."
> - The "Cassia Mountain region" of some reports (Youngquist reconnaissance, 1955 ISMIR bibliography)
>   refers to the South Hills largely in **Twin Falls County**, not to the Albion Range.

## 2. Districts and mining areas in detail

### 2.1 Black Pine district (SE corner of the county)

- **Location/geology:** SE slope of the Black Pine Mountains, T15–16S R29–30E, near I-84 and the Utah
  line. Deposits are irregular **replacements of Mississippian Brazer limestone** along minor fissures,
  in the upper plate of a low-angle thrust over Pennsylvanian Wells Formation. Anderson (1931) classed
  them as **epithermal silver–zinc** (sphalerite, silver-rich tetrahedrite/freibergite, jamesonite,
  minor pyrite, cinnabar, realgar; quartz–barite–calcite gangue) and related them to a young Tertiary
  metallization. Modern work classes the gold system as **Carlin-style, sediment-hosted oxide gold**
  over an ~18 km² footprint.
- **History (lode era):** Ore located ca. 1880. First published report: E.S. Larsen's chapter on
  **cinnabar at Black Pine** in Livingston (1919). Shipments: Silver Hills mine ca. 1894; ~14 cars from
  Hazel Pine ca. 1914; 6 cars of rich zinc ore from the Ruth during WWI. **Silver Hills Mining Co.**
  (Utah corp., filed in Idaho 31 Mar 1920; Busy Bee & Joveon groups, 30–39 unpatented claims) drove
  3 tunnels, a 550-ft shaft, >3,000 ft total; average ore from the upper tunnel assayed **0.03 oz Au,
  42.4 oz Ag, 3.6% Pb, 1.2% Cu, 8.4% Zn, 4.5% Sb** (Anderson 1931); still doing lease work in 1955.
  **Virmyra Gold Mining Co.** (inc. 1940; Denver N. Tallman, pres.) held the Virmyra group, leased to
  the Duvall Company in the 1950s — origin of the "Tallman/Tolman/Duvall/Virmyra" names later applied
  to Pegasus pits. Mercury was prospected at Juniper Hill and the Miller cinnabar property.
- **Pegasus era:** **Noranda Exploration** delineated disseminated gold in the 1980s; **Pegasus Gold
  Corp.** opened the Black Pine open-pit heap-leach mine in **1991**, mined the A, B, CD, E and (NE)
  Tolman pits, poured **over 87,000 oz in 1996 alone** (IGS annual review), and closed in **1997**
  after producing **435,000 oz gold and 189,000 oz silver**. Pegasus Gold went bankrupt in 1998;
  the site was reclaimed.
- **Liberty Gold era (current):** **Liberty Gold Corp.** (then Pilot Gold) bought Black Pine from
  Western Pacific Resources in **June 2016** (US$800k + 300k shares), inheriting 1,874 historic drill
  holes. PFS (Oct 2024): probable reserve **299.4 Mt @ 0.32 g/t = 3.11 Moz Au**; indicated resource
  4.16 Moz + inferred 0.71 Moz; 17-yr run-of-mine heap-leach life; peak 231 koz/yr. Permitting: Mine
  Plan of Operations deemed administratively complete **Nov 2025**; **FAST-41 covered project**
  schedule published Mar 2026; BLM **Notice of Intent (EIS) published 3 Apr 2026**; feasibility study
  targeted **Q4 2026**; first gold targeted **2028**. Status: **advanced exploration/pre-development**.
- **Anchor:** Black Pine Mine site, IGS PO0175, 42.077544 N, −113.042955 W.

### 2.2 Stokes district (Albion Range)

- **Location/geology:** Three clusters: (a) **Conner Creek**, east side of the northern Albion Range
  ~7 mi south of Albion (workings at 7,000–8,200 ft); (b) **Fairchild and Boulder creeks** on the west
  slope east/northeast of Oakley; (c) the **Old Dominion** area 4 mi NE of Albion. Veins are
  **mesothermal silver-lead-quartz fissure fillings** (galena with tetrahedrite, pyrite, chalcopyrite;
  argentiferous) in Precambrian **Harrison-series quartzites**, clustered around a small porphyritic
  granite stock (an outlier of Anderson's "Cassia batholith") near the axis of the range's dome;
  minor replacements in Carboniferous limestone on the west slope. (Modern geology: the Albion Range
  is part of the Raft River–Albion–Grouse Creek **metamorphic core complex**; the Almo pluton is
  Oligocene.)
- **History:** Discovered ca. 1880; worked "in a desultory manner" with small shipments before 1900
  and sporadically after. **Melcher Mining & Milling Co.** (inc. 2 May 1899, McIntyre interests, Salt
  Lake City) was the major operator: Melcher (a.k.a. Cumora) mine on Conner Creek, Sec. 8 T13S R25E,
  two main tunnels 3,300 ft and 1,600 ft, a tram, and a modern flotation mill (4 cells, ball mill,
  Wilfley tables); concentrates were chiefly galena with ~4% Cu, silver values, ~$10/ton gold. Idle
  after 1920. **Big Bertha Mining Co.** (inc. 1924) held 13 claims with 500-ft and 400-ft tunnels.
  Other properties: Albion group (Alice, Badger, Flagstaff, Independence), Golden Eagle, Giant,
  Bimetallic, Harris & Hume, Old Dominion, Walton No. 2. The 1929 Inspector's report lists everything
  idle ("annual labor only"); the district never revived. Nearby **Grape Creek district** (Last Chance
  group near Malta/Elba, J.W. Durfee) appears in 1929 as a separate minor label.
- **Production:** never systematically recorded; Anderson could not obtain Melcher figures. Small.
- **Anchor:** Cumora/Melcher Mine, IGS PO0032, 42.302918 N, −113.554832 W.

### 2.3 Dolomite district (Utah-border silver belt south of Oakley)

- **Verification result:** The **Vipont mine itself is in Utah** (Ashbrook district, Box Elder County,
  Grouse Creek Mountains) — it does not appear anywhere in the IGS Idaho database. But the belt and its
  workforce were tied to Oakley, and Idaho carried its own district name for the Cassia County side:
  the **Dolomite district**, listed in Idaho Mine Inspector reports 1921–1955 ("the high grade
  lead-silver and silver-copper ores of the Stokes and Dolomite districts").
- **Idaho-side operators (1920s):**
  - **Idaho Silver Mining Co.** (Oakley; W.M. Garvin pres.; inc. 2 Aug 1920): 13 unpatented claims
    "part of which lies in Dolomite dist., Idaho, and Ashbrook dist., Utah; Vipont, Utah...near and in
    the same belt with the famous Vipont mine"; ~300 ft of underground silver-copper workings, avg 2 men
    (1922 report). = IGS record PO0062 inside Cassia County.
  - **Oakley Mining & Milling Co.** (John H. Fairchild): Boulder M group, 7 claims, Dolomite dist.
  - **Little Jewel** mine (George Myers, Oakley), carried under "Dolomite" 1921–1923.
- **The Vipont connection:** silver discovered in the 1870s–1880s; the Vipont's boom came 1917–1923
  under Vipont Mining Co. — in **1922** it mined 63,710 tons averaging **16.3 oz/ton Ag** and 0.046
  oz/ton Au (≈1.0 M oz silver that year), making it one of Utah's leading silver mines; a US Bureau of
  Mines drilling program in the 1950s blocked out reserves that were then uneconomic, and a new mill
  reworked dumps in the late 1970s. Freight and crews moved through Oakley, 20 miles north.
- **Status:** abandoned on the Idaho side; no recorded Idaho production.
- **Anchor:** Idaho Silver Mining Co. site, IGS PO0062, 42.01286 N, −113.858406 W (~1.5 km north of
  the state line).

### 2.4 Goose Creek district (lignite; uranium; failed oil tests)

- **Location/geology:** Goose Creek plateau, SW Cassia County, Tps. 14–16 S., Rs. 20–22 E. (~156 sq mi
  in Bowen's outline; ~260 sq mi in the USGS 1950s study spanning into Utah/Nevada). Lignite beds lie
  in late Miocene(?)–Pliocene fluviatile-lacustrine-pyroclastic beds (Payette/Salt Lake formations)
  beneath rhyolite rimrock.
- **History:** Coal noted as early as the Union Pacific's 1888 book *Idaho* ("10 beds of coal 3.5 to
  18 feet thick" on Goose, Trapper and Grouse creeks); state inspector R.N. Bell described the lignite
  area ca. 1907. **Bowen (USGS, 1911 fieldwork)** mapped two beds — the **Worthington bed** (3–5 ft,
  the only one mined) and the dirty **Barrett bed**. The **Worthington mine** (SE¼ sec. 26, T16S R20E,
  Beaverdam basin; slope driven ~300 ft) was "the only one in the district that has marketed any coal":
  operated **one winter** a few years before 1911, product hauled 25 miles by team to Oakley; the fuel
  disappointed (as-received: 34.5% moisture, 18.0% ash, 5,810 Btu) and the mine never reopened. Other
  prospects: Lang, Barrett, Grant, Smith, Eliason, plus openings on Trapper Creek.
- **Uranium phase:** For the AEC, **Mapel & Hail (1953–1955)** documented **uranium-bearing
  carbonaceous shale and lignite** across the Goose Creek district (USGS TEI-339, TEI-438); IGS DD-1
  still carries paired "coal, uranium" records (Barrett Mine, Worthington Mine, Grant Prospect). No
  production.
- **Oil & gas:** ca. 1919–1929 several ventures drilled the basin and nearby valleys: Western **Pearl
  Oil Co.** (standard rig 12 mi above Oakley; 1,670 ft; hit a strong artesian flow, no oil — 1920
  Inspector's report), **Goose Creek Oil & Developing Co.** (Pittsburgh; federal permit on Coal and
  Goose creeks; 1,750-ft well by 1929), **Oasis Oil Co.** (Raft River basin 6 mi south of Almo, 443 ft,
  1920), plus Marsh Basin (Albion) and Malta No. 1 wells. All dry; all idle by the mid-1920s.
- **Anchor:** Worthington Mine, IGS TF0115, 42.011768 N, −114.072432 W.

### 2.5 Oakley stone quarries, Middle Mountain (ACTIVE)

- **Product/geology:** "Oakley stone" (trade name **Rocky Mountain quartzite**; also sold as Sunrise
  Gold, Silver, and green **Elba Quartzite** varieties) is a strongly foliated **micaceous quartzite**
  from the Neoproterozoic **Elba Quartzite** and associated metaquartzites of the Albion Range
  metamorphic core complex, splitting along mica folia into thin natural veneer, flagstone and ledge
  stone with a distinctive mica sheen. Quarried on the **west slope of Middle Mountain**, ~8 mi south
  of Oakley (staging ~5,600 ft elevation).
- **History/status:** Anderson (1931) catalogued the county's building stones (tuff and quartz latite
  used in Albion Normal School and Oakley buildings; quartzite "inexhaustible") before the industry
  existed. **Commercial quarrying began in 1948**, with Gary Mullard trucking and selling stone from
  1952; by the 1980s it sold in 20+ states and Canada at premium prices. Today about **a dozen
  companies** quarry around Oakley. **Northern Stone Supply, Inc.** (in IGS DD-1 as PO0276) operates
  ~1,300 acres of quarries, ships ~**50,000 tons/yr** (April–October season), holds ANSI/NSC 373
  platinum sustainability certification, and the industry generates an estimated **$80–90 M/yr** of
  regional economic activity. Other IGS-recorded operations: Oakley Valley Stone Inc. (Valley View
  Mine, PO0051), Rocky Mountain Quartzite Quarry (PO0055).
- **Anchor:** Oakley Valley Stone / Valley View quarry, IGS PO0051, 42.118769 N, −113.848827 W.

### 2.6 City of Rocks – Almo area (pegmatites, uranium/REE black sands, tungsten)

- **Geology:** Around Almo, the Oligocene **Almo pluton** (granite of the City of Rocks spires)
  intrudes Archean **Green Creek Complex** basement overlain by the Neoproterozoic **Elba Quartzite**
  and schists (Miller et al., 2008, USGS OFR 2008-1103). Pegmatites fringe the pluton and basement.
- **Prospecting history:** small prospect pits for **pegmatite mica** (sought as insulation material),
  **feldspar** and **beryl**; the **City of Rocks Mine** (PO0069) is recorded by MRDS as a small past
  producer of feldspar (with beryllium/uranium noted); the **White Mica Mining and Milling** prospect
  (PO0071) carries Ag-Au-Pb-U-Cu-Be-Zn-Th in IGS records. Anderson (1931) reported mica prospecting at
  the south end of South Mountain (books rarely >4 in., not commercial) and pegmatites near the Utah
  line. In the 1950s uranium boom, the **Brooks prospect / "Wicked Witch" City of Rocks uranium
  deposit** (PO0065) and the **City of Rocks uranium placer** (PO0066) were staked, and **black-sand
  placers** carrying monazite–magnetite–zircon–ilmenite–garnet (iron, REE, Th, Ti, Zr in the records)
  were evaluated along **Edwards, Graham and Circle creeks and the upper Raft River in the Almo
  Basin** (IGS tags two of these "Stokes"; MRDS lists monazite as commodity #1, source: IGS mineral
  property files). None produced commercially.
- **Tungsten:** the **Black Jack Scheelite** occurrence near Almo (PO0070) is the county's only
  recorded tungsten showing; the statewide context report is Livingston (1919), *Tungsten, cinnabar,
  molybdenum and tin deposits of Idaho*.
- **Status:** the **City of Rocks National Reserve** (est. 18 Nov 1988) closed the core area to
  mineral entry; NPS's Abandoned Mineral Lands database (2010 GRI report) lists **seven AML features
  at six sites** in the reserve — dozer trenches, small surface mines, and one plugged oil/gas well.
- **Anchor:** City of Rocks Mine, IGS PO0069, 42.04737 N, −113.735424 W.

### 2.7 Snake River – Raft River fine-gold placers (northern county edge)

- **Character:** The Snake River's famous "flour gold" — 0.930–0.951 fine, particles so small that
  **1,000–2,000 colors are worth one cent** (Hill 1916). Worked on skim bars and low terraces.
- **History:** 1880s rushes worked bars from Blackfoot to Glenns Ferry. Bancroft (1890) records **Lane
  & Co. near the mouth of Raft River** (Cassia County) making $25 per man-day. Recorded USGS statistics
  1902–1915 credit **Cassia County with $57,408** of $149,315 total for the upper Snake — the largest
  county share. Placer work "between Burley and Milner" ended when **Milner Dam (1905)** backwater
  submerged the bars, and the **Minidoka reservoir** drowned others. Big mechanized schemes (burlap
  tables, dredges near Minidoka such as the Sweetzer–Burroughs dredge) failed to save the fine gold.
  IGS DD-1 records the Bancroft, Durango, Sweetzer Farm (a recorded producer), G.W. Lish Farm and
  Spring Bar placers along the Raft/Snake confluence reach, and Goose Creek/Snake River placers near
  Burley.
- **Anchor:** Raft River (Sweetzer Farm) Placer, IGS PO0097, 42.611267 N, −113.241816 W.

### 2.8 Minor industrial-mineral notes (not treated as districts)

- **Marble/lime:** Precambrian marble quarried and burned for lime at several points along the west
  and north Albion Range (Albion "Idaho Stone and Lime Co." quarry PO0018; Brittannic Mining Co. quarry
  near Declo PO0011; kilns on Land Creek and near Oakley/Basin — Anderson 1931, p. 153–157).
- **Brick clay:** Burley Clay Mine (PO0007) — MRDS lists it as a **producer** of brick clay.
- **Pumicite (volcanic ash):** widespread in the Tertiary section (7 IGS occurrences); Anderson judged
  it a large unexploited resource.
- **Kyanite:** Bill Anderson kyanite property NE of Albion (PO0013); never commercial.
- **Geothermal:** MRDS lists ~60 geothermal occurrences/wells in the county (Oakley warm springs,
  Artesian City margin, Raft River KGRA edges) — outside mining scope but dominant in the MRDS count.

## 4. Geologic setting (summary)

- **Albion Range** (with the Raft River and Grouse Creek ranges) is a **metamorphic core complex**:
  Archean Green Creek Complex basement, Neoproterozoic Elba Quartzite and schist cover (Anderson's
  "Harrison series"), Paleozoic carbonates in upper plates, all intruded by the ~Oligocene **Almo
  pluton** and older Cretaceous(?) granite (Anderson's "Cassia batholith"); Miocene–Pliocene Salt
  Lake/Payette basin fill and rhyolite/quartz-latite volcanics flank the ranges; Basin-Range normal
  faulting is young (Anderson argued early Pleistocene). Stokes-district veins cluster at the granite
  stock in the quartzite dome; Oakley stone is the sheared, micaceous facies of the Elba Quartzite on
  Middle Mountain.
- **Black Pine Mountains**: thrust-stacked Mississippian–Pennsylvanian(–Permian) carbonate and
  clastic rocks (Brazer limestone over Wells Formation in Anderson's usage). Historic Ag-Zn-Hg
  replacements and the modern Carlin-style oxide gold system (jasperoidal quartz, fine pyrite,
  realgar/cinnabar association) share the same carbonate host belt.
- **Goose Creek basin**: Miocene–Pliocene tuffaceous lake/stream beds with lignite, capped by rhyolite;
  gentle anticline exposes the coal on Beaverdam/Trapper creeks; uranium is adsorbed in carbonaceous
  beds.
- **Snake River placers**: flour gold in Quaternary bar and terrace gravels, likely reworked from
  far-upstream sources; too fine for mechanical recovery.
